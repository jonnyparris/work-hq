We keep saying agents are like very good interns that need a lot of steering. What if your intern was just very good at asking — for help, for context, for a decision? Notes that surface themselves, instead of you chasing them. (Someone at BUPK called it "AI interns to AI secretaries".)

The middle of the screen is one question: what do you need from me? One key to answer. The options fit the task — a blog post gets open / publish / revise / schedule; a ticket gets file it / not mine. And there is an end: clear it and you get a calm screen. It's live-connected to memory, meeting transcripts, Gmail, calendar and cmux, where the agents actually run. Things pop up when they're needed and when they're done. Every answer goes in the journal, and the journal outranks stale notes — so it keeps updating the context and stops bothering me about things that are finished.

Say pineapple.

The capture side. Say "pineapple" on a call and the last couple of minutes become a task, with the exact quote. Meeting notes get swept every 30 minutes as a backstop.

⌘K → an agent you can see.

⌘K  draft a blog post on how much I love cheese
π Draft · how much I love cheese
● mcp wiki search "cheese"
● write scratch/blog-drafts/cheese.md

Anything fresh goes in the command bar and an agent is sent off on the fly. It runs in a cmux tab I can watch or take over — and I almost never need to. That's the fix for "I don't know what it's doing or where it's running". It's become my cockpit for all things work: I dispatch from here and it writes the prompts better than I would.

For the engineers: I don't want a model deciding when to interrupt me. The "when to ask" rules are boring, tested code. The model does the fuzzy part — what a transcript means, what I meant, and the actual work.
